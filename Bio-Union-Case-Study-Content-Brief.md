## 1. Purpose

This document translates Bio Union's 2023 pitch deck into a recruiter-facing product case study. It is not a rewrite of the pitch deck and must not read like an investor presentation.

The page should demonstrate:

- Product vision across garments, sensors, hardware, software, and service design
- Systems thinking
- Wearable-technology and textile-engineering depth
- Cross-functional leadership
- Product and business-model development
- Ability to work across manufacturing, clinical, software, and commercialization constraints
- Mature reflection on an ambitious venture that did not continue

The case study should make Bio Union relevant to product design, creative technology, connected-product, innovation, and design-leadership roles.

## 4. What the pitch deck establishes

### Founder and venture

The deck identifies Kai Han "Jacky" Ho as Bio Union's founder and manager and as a fashion-design instructor at George Brown College (deck page 2).

### Problem framing

The deck frames a broad health-monitoring problem through:

- Cardiovascular disease
- Sleep and insomnia
- Aging populations
- Time and cost within conventional medical workflows
- Limitations of wrist-worn consumer wearables for some forms of sustained measurement

Relevant deck pages: 4-11.

The portfolio should compress this into one focused product problem instead of reproducing the entire public-health argument.

### Product thesis

The deck describes Bio Union as a multi-sensor "dressable" technology platform for near-real-time vital-sign analysis and monitoring, based on the premise that textiles:

- cover much of the body;
- are present throughout daily life;
- can place sensors closer to useful body locations;
- can connect body data with digital services.

Relevant deck pages: 13-14.

### Product concept

The deck presents the Leap M ECG smart-garment concept and a proposed mobile experience. Illustrated capability areas include:

- Heart rate
- Heart-rate variability
- Activity
- Breathing rate
- Lung-capacity-related monitoring
- Stress
- Fatigue
- Abnormal heart-rhythm alerting
- Sleep analysis
- Blood oxygen

Relevant deck page: 15.

These are proposed or represented product capabilities. The public case study must not imply that every capability was implemented, validated, or medically approved.

### ECG-centered approach

The deck positions chest-based ECG sensing as the central technology and contrasts it with optical PPG sensing commonly used in wrist wearables.

Relevant deck pages: 9-11 and 17-18.

The portfolio should describe this as a design and product hypothesis:

> The team explored whether electrodes integrated into a close-fitting garment could support sustained ECG capture with less dependence on a user holding or positioning a separate device.

Avoid absolute statements that all smartwatches are inaccurate or that ECG clothing is automatically diagnostic.

### Garment design and sensing

The deck shows front and back photographs of a single-lead ECG/EKG garment and describes:

- Conductive printed elements
- A garment pattern intended to stabilize device and electrode placement during movement
- A fit described as close but not excessively tight
- A removable device attached at the chest
- A noise-filtering algorithm
- An ECG report concept

Relevant deck page: 19.

### Hardware evolution

The deck documents two Leap M device generations:

**Leap M Gen.2**

- Dated 2019 through the deck's 2023 "present"
- Listed eight-hour battery life
- Listed two-hour charging time

**Leap M Gen.3**

- Dated 2022
- Described as ready for mass production in the 2023 deck
- Listed weight under 13 grams
- Listed battery life above 600 hours
- Listed 30-metre water resistance

The same slide lists ISO 10993, RoHS, CE, FCC, IP67, ECG sample rate of 250 Hz+, independent-lab clinical validation, Canadian design, and Taiwanese manufacturing.

Relevant deck page: 20.

All specifications and compliance claims require evidence before public use. If not verified, present the hardware timeline visually without the unverified badges and numbers.

### Long-term monitoring rationale

The deck cites a 2007 review in the *Journal of Thrombosis and Thrombolysis* to support the importance of ambulatory ECG data during daily activity.

Relevant deck page: 21.

This may inform the project rationale, but the portfolio should not reproduce the deck's medical interpretation without reviewing the original paper and confirming that the summary is accurate.

### Market and competition

The deck contains:

- Telehealth and wearable medical-device market forecasts
- A smart-clothing CAGR slide
- A competitive-positioning matrix

Relevant deck pages: 22-28.

These slides show that the venture considered market size and positioning. Their forecasts are now dated and should not be published as current evidence. A concise "2023 business framing" section is sufficient.

### Strategic relationships

The deck identifies four strategic relationships:

- Makalot - clothing manufacturer
- ventureLAB - startup accelerator/ecosystem
- Clinic+ - clinical relationship
- Riversoft - AI data analysis and NLP development

Relevant deck page: 29.

The page should describe the type of cross-disciplinary ecosystem the venture assembled. Partner names and logos should be published only after confirming the relationship wording, dates, logo rights, and whether "strategic partner" remains accurate historically.

### Product principles and competitive advantages

The deck lists six intended advantages:

- Ease of use
- Comfort
- Wireless integration
- Cost/time savings
- Multiple use cases
- Reduced dependence on disposable patches

Relevant deck page: 30.

For the portfolio, convert these from marketing claims into design principles and hypotheses. For example:

- Reduce setup burden
- Integrate sensors into a familiar garment
- Minimize wires and disposable components
- Design for repeated use
- Support multiple monitoring contexts

### Team

The deck shows a multidisciplinary team and advisory structure across:

- Founder and product leadership
- Software engineering
- iOS/macOS engineering
- Sports science
- Finance
- Industrial design
- UI/UX design
- Asia business development
- Biomedical engineering
- Hardware engineering

Relevant deck page: 32.

Use this to demonstrate cross-functional coordination. Publish individual names only after confirming role accuracy and consent. The deck marks one hardware co-founder as a former member.

### Business model

The deck explores a B2B2C structure with:

- ODM/licensing relationships with clothing brands
- A Bio Union-branded product and optional annual system subscription
- Licensing/subscription
- Service-provider relationships with clinics, rehabilitation centres, elderly-care providers, remote monitoring, pharmacies, home care, insurers, employers, gyms, and other specialized users
- Consumer audiences including chronic/high-risk patients, older adults living independently, newly diagnosed people, and sport users

Relevant deck pages: 33-34.

The case study should present this as service and commercialization exploration, not as proven revenue.

### Public presence

The deck documents:

- 2017 MEDICA Connected Healthcare Forum in Dusseldorf
- 2018 press and MOOK Magazine interviews
- 2020-2021 IFAH "Top 100 Healthcare Leader Awards"
- 2021 online interview about technology and textile engineering
- 2022 Virtual Internships Global Employability Expert Series guest speaking

Relevant deck pages: 35-39.

Each item requires a link, program, image permission, certificate, recording, or other verification before publication. A few verified highlights are stronger than a large logo wall.

## 5. Required case-study page architecture

The Bio Union page should be more extensive than the standard project template because it must explain a connected physical-digital system and the venture's evolution.

### Section 1 - Hero

**Goal:** Establish the project in under 15 seconds.

Include:

- Bio Union
- Headline
- Historical-status label
- Jacky's role
- Date range
- Capabilities
- Original garment or device image

Recommended copy:

> Bio Union explored a connected smart-garment system designed to support long-term ECG monitoring through clothing. I founded the venture and led product development across textiles, wearable hardware, digital experience, partner coordination, and commercialization strategy.

Do not lead with "AI and blockchain." The deck does not provide enough system detail for blockchain to be a credible central case-study theme.

### Section 2 - At a glance

Use a five-item facts panel:

- **Challenge:** Make sustained physiological monitoring more wearable in daily life.
- **Product:** Single-lead ECG smart garment, removable sensing device, and connected software concept.
- **Role:** Founder / Product Development Lead - confirm.
- **Team:** Multidisciplinary contributors across garment, hardware, software, design, biomedical engineering, and business.
- **Status:** Historical venture documented through 2023; not a currently available product.

### Section 3 - The human and system problem

Focus the problem:

> Health data is often captured in short clinical windows or through consumer devices that may not be designed for every monitoring need. Bio Union asked whether a familiar object - clothing - could make longer-duration sensing easier to integrate into everyday routines.

Supporting topics:

- Need for longer-duration information during ordinary activity
- Friction of separate electrodes, wires, patches, and setup
- Comfort and adherence
- Connection between home data and professional follow-up

Do not reproduce multiple fear-based disease statistics. One properly sourced statement is sufficient if it materially supports the design problem.

### Section 4 - The product thesis: clothing as an interface

Explain the core idea:

- Textiles contact a large area of the body.
- Garments are already part of daily routines.
- Garment pattern and material decisions can influence sensor placement and stability.
- A removable device can separate electronics from washable textile components.
- A connected application can translate captured signals into a usable experience.

Recommended visual:

`Body → garment/electrodes → Leap M device → signal processing → mobile/report → user or professional follow-up`

Create a new accessible system diagram based on deck pages 13-20 and 34. Caption it:

> System diagram reconstructed in 2026 from Bio Union's 2023 product architecture.

### Section 5 - Why ECG in a garment?

Frame this as a decision, not a technology superiority claim.

Cover:

- The deck's distinction between wrist PPG and chest ECG
- Sensor location and sustained capture as design considerations
- The desire to reduce deliberate measurement steps
- The trade-off between a close-fitting garment, comfort, movement, washability, and signal quality

Recommended heading:

**Choosing a sensing approach around the monitoring context**

Avoid:

- "Smart watches are inaccurate"
- "ECG is always better"
- "Diagnosis" unless supported and legally appropriate

### Section 6 - Designing the garment around movement

Use deck page 19 as primary visual evidence.

Explain:

- Single-lead garment construction
- Printed conductive paths/electrodes
- Front and back placement
- Chest-mounted removable device
- Pattern structure intended to limit movement at the sensing area
- Close but comfortable fit as an explicit design goal
- Wash/reuse considerations

Add:

- Original photos
- Pattern or electrode diagrams if available
- Early iteration sketches
- Material tests
- A short "design constraints" panel

Suggested constraint panel:

| Constraint | Product implication |
|---|---|
| Signal contact | Electrode placement and garment fit matter |
| Movement | Structure must reduce sensor displacement and noise |
| Comfort | Garment cannot rely on excessive compression |
| Care | Electronics should be removable from the washable textile |
| Repeat use | Materials and conductive elements must tolerate repeated wear and care |
| Inclusion | Sizing, body variation, skin sensitivity, and gendered fit require testing |

The final three implications require evidence of actual testing before being described as achieved.

### Section 15 - Outcome and the pandemic chapter

The pitch deck does not fully document why Bio Union ended. This section requires Jacky's first-person account.

Recommended framing:

The venture nevertheless produced a connected-product architecture, physical prototypes, cross-disciplinary partnerships, and a deeper understanding of the gap between an ambitious technical vision and a focused path to validation and market entry.

This wording requires Jacky's approval.

### Section 16 - What I learned

Recommended lessons:

- Connected products require concurrent validation of the garment, hardware, signal, software, and service model.
- Comfort and repeat use are product requirements, not cosmetic details.
- Medical and wellness claims must be separated early.
- Technical breadth needs a narrow initial user and use case.
- Partnerships need explicit deliverables, evidence, ownership, and timelines.
- Hardware readiness, regulatory readiness, and market readiness are different states.

### Section 17 - How I would approach it today

Recommended retrospective:

- Select one monitoring scenario and one primary user.
- Define the decision the data must support.
- Validate electrode contact, movement artifacts, comfort, sizing, and care before expanding features.
- Separate wellness functionality from regulated medical functionality.
- Build traceable evidence requirements alongside product requirements.
- Design the clinician or service-provider workflow as carefully as the wearer experience.
- Create staged technical, regulatory, manufacturing, and commercialization gates.
- Use AI only where there is a validated data, safety, and human-review model.

Label this:

> 2026 retrospective - not part of the original Bio Union product.

## 10. Medical and legal copy guardrails

- "Designed to save lives"
- "Prevents sudden incidents"
- "Treats progress"
- "Diagnosis"
- "Medical grade"
- "Clinically validated"
- "Standard of care"
- "Accurate" as an unqualified comparison
- "24/7 monitoring" unless technically and operationally supported
- "Ready for mass production" as a current status

Preferred language:

- "explored"
- "designed to support"
- "product hypothesis"
- "prototype"
- "represented capability"
- "historical specification"
- "the 2023 deck documented"
- "requires verification"
- "not currently offered for clinical use"
